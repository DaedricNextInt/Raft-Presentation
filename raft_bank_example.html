    Speaker notes:

    "Imagine a bank running five Vault servers across different locations. These servers form one Vault cluster. When the bank updates a database password, the Vault leader encrypts the data and uses Raft to replicate the storage operation to the other servers. Once a majority of servers have recorded the entry, Raft can commit it."

    "If the leader crashes, the remaining servers can elect a new leader and continue operating. If too many servers fail and quorum is lost, Raft stops committing new writes rather than risk conflicting changes. This is why Vault uses Raft: to keep persistent data consistent and available even when individual servers fail."

    Locations are illustrative. Real deployments consider network latency and failure domains. Simplified: real Raft also checks log freshness and term-specific commit rules.
