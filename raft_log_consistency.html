LOG REPLICATION

How Raft Keeps Data Consistent

Log append means the Raft leader adds a new record to its own list of operations before replicating that record to other servers. Think of the Raft log as a numbered journal that records storage operations in order. Our cluster: five Vault nodes, S4 is the leader.

THREE TERMS TO REMEMBER

1 · AppendRecord a new operation in the log.
2 · ReplicateSend that log entry to other nodes.
3 · CommitConfirm that the operation is part of the cluster's agreed history.
Appending an entry locally is not the same as committing it across the cluster.
Speaker notes:

"When a client writes a secret to Vault, the Raft leader first records the encrypted storage operation in its local Raft log. This is called a log append. The entry isn't committed yet. The leader must replicate it to a majority of voting nodes before it can be committed."

The entry contains the storage operation and associated data, not the plaintext password. Once committed, the operation becomes part of the cluster's agreed history, and Raft applies committed operations to its storage state in log order. Every healthy node eventually applies the same committed operations in the same order.

Educational visualization: simplified. Commit also requires the entry to be from the leader's current term (here term 2) and Raft's other commit conditions to be satisfied.
